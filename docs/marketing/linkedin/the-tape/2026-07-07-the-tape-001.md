THE TAPE #001 — fictional candidate, real mistake.

First round. Phone. Minute six.

Interviewer: "Depreciation goes up by $10. Walk me through the three statements."
Candidate: "Sure — net income falls by $10..."
[notes: no tax rate. waiting.]
Candidate: "...then on the cash flow statement I add back the $10, since it's non-cash. So cash is flat."
[notes: 'flat.' still no tax shield. two statements in.]
Interviewer: "Flat. Then why do companies fight for accelerated depreciation?"
Candidate: "...for the accounting benefit?"
[notes: that was the lifeline. pass.]
Interviewer: "Okay. Let's move to valuation."

Where it died: the tax line — everything after was just momentum.

What the tape should have said:
At a 40% tax rate (round numbers), pre-tax income falls $10, so net income falls $6 — not $10.
On the cash flow statement, start at −$6, add back the full $10 of non-cash depreciation: cash is UP $4.
Balance sheet: cash +$4, PP&E −$10, assets down $6 — matching retained earnings. It ties.
Depreciation shields taxes. That $4 is the entire point of the question.
